## 1. Retention / decay accuracy

**Method.** `EbbinghausEngine.compute_retention(t, S)` compared with the closed form R = e^(−t/S) over a grid of 10 stabilities × 12 elapsed times (S ∈ [1, 6, 24, 31.2, 45.6, 60, 72, 168, 720, 8760] h; t ∈ [0, 0.5, 1, 6, 12, 24, 48, 72, 168, 720, 2000, 8760] h). Then the full production path, `Episode.retention(now)` (datetime timestamps + per-agent decay multiplier m), against e^(−t·m/S).

Characteristic points (S = 45.6 h shown; 24 h and 72 h identical):

| Identity | Engine | Theory |
|---|---|---|
| R(0) | 1.000000 | 1.000000 |
| R(S) = 1/e | 0.367879 | 0.367879 |
| R(S·ln2) = 0.5 | 0.500000 | 0.500000 |
| R(S·ln(1/θ)) = θ | 0.300000 | 0.300000 |

Forgetting threshold θ = 0.3, so a memory is forgotten at t* = S·ln(1/θ) ≈ 1.204·S. `is_forgotten()` flips within ±0.01 h of t* for every tested S, and `time_until_forgotten()` predicts t* exactly.

**Documented clamps (by design, not errors).** Negative elapsed time returns R = 1.0. Stability above MAX_STABILITY_HOURS = 8760 h is treated as the cap:

| S (h) | t (h) | Engine | Pure e^(−t/S) | e^(−t/cap) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10000 | 720 | 0.9211 | 0.9305 | 0.9211 |
| 10000 | 8760 | 0.3679 | 0.4164 | 0.3679 |
| 17520 | 720 | 0.9211 | 0.9597 | 0.9211 |
| 17520 | 8760 | 0.3679 | 0.6065 | 0.3679 |

*Practical note:* `add()` gives new memories S between 31.2 h and 60.0 h, and each `recall()` multiplies S by ~1.5–1.7 up to the same 8760 h cap, so S itself never exceeds the cap. The clamp only changes results for an agent with a decay multiplier < 1 once a memory has been recalled enough for S/m to pass 8760 h.

## 2. Caregiver alert precision / recall

**Method.** 20 synthetic memories stored through the real `MemoryStore`, each aged to a target retention by moving `last_reviewed_at` back (the same mutation `/simulate_time` performs), then scored with `detect_alerts()`. Each memory carries a caregiver-intent label written before running the detector: "should a caregiver be alerted about this fading memory?"

Scored three ways:

- **(a) Rule conformance:** does `detect_alerts` implement its documented rule (critical keyword AND R < 0.5; critical if R < 0.3, else warning)? The rule is re-implemented independently in the script as the reference.
- **(b) Caregiver intent:** how well that rule matches what a caregiver actually wants.
- **(c) End-to-end:** one real `MemoryScheduler` maintenance cycle with the caregiver hook wired as in `Orchestrator.check_caregiver_alerts`; counts what was actually notified.

**Reading the numbers.**

- The implementation is exactly its spec (20/20).
- Against intent, the 4 false positives are all substring matches: "son" inside person / season / reason, "home" inside homework. The 3 false negatives are critical information with none of the 13 keywords (pills, nurse, a phone number). "grandson" is a true positive only by the same substring accident.
- End-to-end recall is lower than (b) because 5 critical-level memories were deleted by the maintenance cycle's decay/cleanup *before* its alert hook ran (the ordering issue found in Phase 6), so they were never notified: "my daughter Priya brings my medicine"; "insulin before breakfast every day"; "met a nice person at the park"; "autumn is my favourite season"; "the reason I moved here was the climate".
- (c)'s higher precision (0.80) is not an improvement: the same pre-alert pruning that drops real critical alerts also drops the critical-level false positives. Only warning-level alerts (0.3 ≤ R < 0.5) can reach a caregiver on the scheduled path.
- Scheduler failures during the run: 0.

## 3. Feedback-loop convergence (Phase 1)

**Method.** Signals sent through the real public API (`MemoryStore.record_feedback` / `record_task_outcome` → `MemoryOrchestrator` → `EpisodicMemoryStore`, persisted to SQLite). Each update is an EMA step with α = 0.3 from the 0.5 default, so after n identical signals the value should be exactly 1 − 0.5·0.7ⁿ (positive) or 0.5·0.7ⁿ (negative).

**(a) Repeated identical signals, 50 each:**

| Field | Signal | Max error vs closed form | Value after 50 | Signals to within 0.1 / 0.01 / 0.001 | Monotone | In [0,1] | Survives reload |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| agent_feedback | positive | 1.1e-16 | 1.00e+00 | 5 / 11 / 18 | yes | yes | yes |
| agent_feedback | negative | 2.8e-17 | 8.99e-09 | 5 / 11 / 18 | yes | yes | yes |
| task_success_rate | positive | 1.1e-16 | 1.00e+00 | 5 / 11 / 18 | yes | yes | yes |
| task_success_rate | negative | 2.8e-17 | 8.99e-09 | 5 / 11 / 18 | yes | yes | yes |

**(b) Noisy signals.** 600 random signals drawn with probability p; statistics over the last 500 (first 100 discarded as burn-in). An EMA's long-run mean equals the rate of signals it received, with standard deviation √(α/(2−α)·p(1−p)). The reference is the rate actually sent: with 500 draws that rate itself wanders about ±0.02 around p, which is sampling noise, not EMA error.

| Field | p | Rate actually sent | EMA mean | EMA − sent | Observed SD | Theoretical SD |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| agent_feedback | 0.2 | 0.210 | 0.208 | -0.002 | 0.168 | 0.168 |
| agent_feedback | 0.5 | 0.558 | 0.559 | +0.001 | 0.220 | 0.210 |
| agent_feedback | 0.8 | 0.762 | 0.762 | -0.000 | 0.173 | 0.168 |
| task_success_rate | 0.2 | 0.218 | 0.217 | -0.001 | 0.161 | 0.168 |
| task_success_rate | 0.5 | 0.498 | 0.497 | -0.001 | 0.211 | 0.210 |
| task_success_rate | 0.8 | 0.772 | 0.774 | +0.002 | 0.173 | 0.168 |

**(c) Alternating +/−, 200 signals:** settles into a fixed 2-cycle 0.5882 / 0.4118 (theory 0.5882 / 0.4118). The value is bounded and does not drift.

**(d) Effect on the DMS priority score** (identical memories, importance 0.45, same age, private weights): baseline 0.409; after 20 positive + 20 successful signals 0.729 (+78%); after 20 negative + 20 failed signals 0.088 (-78%).

**Interpretation.** One signal moves the value 30% of the way to its target; 11 consistent signals bring it within 0.01. Under noisy signals the long-run value tracks the rate of signals received to within 0.002, while fluctuating around it with SD 0.16–0.22 from turn to turn. Note that `PlannerAgent` records a *positive* signal every time it retrieves a memory that passed the relevance cut-off and was used in a turn whose LLM call succeeded (Phase 9; previously every top-k hit, even on failed turns), so `agent_feedback` now tracks how often a memory is genuinely useful. `task_success_rate` moves only through `POST /feedback`.
